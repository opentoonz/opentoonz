Print Process

Simulate offset printing for comic, retro and risograph looks. Add Print Process from the Stylize category and connect the image to Source. The image is separated into cyan, magenta, yellow and black plates; each is printed through its own dot screen, and the inks are recombined on paper.

Screen

Cell Size is the screen's cell in the drawing's own pixels: small cells read as fine printing, large ones as pop-art dots. Dot Shape is Round, Diamond or Line; for every shape a tone inks exactly its share of each cell. Black Generation is the share of the common grey printed with the black plate instead of three inks. Dot Gain spreads the ink, darkening the middle tones as soft paper does. Dot Softness is the width of the dot edges in pixels; 0 gives hard dots. Output shows the print, or one plate in black on white.

Plates

Each plate can be turned off and has its own screen Angle (the classic 15, 75, 0 and 45 degrees keep the dots from forming moiré), its Ink colour and its Offset, the misregistration: the plate's image and dots are moved together by that many pixels, as when the paper slips between colours. Change the inks to fluorescent pink, blue and yellow for a risograph look.

Paper

Background is Paper, printed over the whole frame, or Transparent, keeping the source's transparency for compositing. Paper Colour is the paper under the inks. Paper Grain textures the paper and roughens the dot edges, like ink soaking into fibres; Grain Size is its scale and Seed picks its pattern.

Notes

Every value can be animated: animating an offset makes the plates drift. The screens are tied to the drawing's position, so the dots stay put when the camera does, and the result does not depend on render tiles. 8-bit, 16-bit and floating-point images are supported.
